Overall 91.7%; best BR 95.5%, worst TH 89.5% (Other not counted): a **6.0-point gap**.

**Findings**

- **Representation bias:** US alone is 44.9% of drawings, above the 20.0% cap. A recognizer trained on this learns mostly how people in US draw a cat or a house.
- **Performance gap:** the game's own model recognizes drawings from TH (89.5%) less often than from BR (95.5%). Part of that is drawing style, which is exactly what our recognizer must handle for children everywhere.

**Mitigation: capped sample weights** (`capped_weights`)

No country may hold more than 20.0% of the training weight. Over-represented countries are down-weighted; every other drawing keeps weight 1, so no data is dropped and small countries are not inflated by huge weights.

| country | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| US | 44.9% | 20.0% |
| GB | 7.2% | 10.4% |
| CA | 3.7% | 5.3% |
| DE | 3.3% | 4.8% |
| AU | 2.4% | 3.5% |
| RU | 2.3% | 3.3% |
| BR | 1.9% | 2.7% |
| SE | 1.7% | 2.5% |
| FI | 1.7% | 2.5% |
| CZ | 1.6% | 2.3% |

US share of the training signal: 44.9% → 20.0%. The recognizer team passes these weights to the loss (or sampler) and re-measures the per-country recognition gap on the trained model with the same `MetricFrame` slices (KAN-80).

**Trade-off:** the effective training set shrinks (the down-weighted rows count for less), so overall accuracy may dip slightly in exchange for more even per-country accuracy.

## 2. Stories by lead-character gender

20,754 of 54,642 stories analysed (every picture book plus a fixed random sample of TinyStories). A story's lead is male or female when its gendered words and common names (he/she, boy/girl, Tom/Lily, ...) lean that way; otherwise neutral.

| source | female | male | neutral |
|---|---|---|---|
| african_storybook | 31.7% | 51.0% | 17.4% |
| storyweaver | 37.3% | 51.1% | 11.5% |
| tinystories | 46.2% | 47.4% | 6.4% |

**Findings**

- `african_storybook`: 51.0% male vs 31.7% female leads, skews **male** by 19 points.
- `storyweaver`: 51.1% male vs 37.3% female leads, skews **male** by 14 points.
- `tinystories`: 47.4% male vs 46.2% female leads, roughly balanced.

**Mitigation: gender-swap augmentation, per source** (`gender_swap`, `balance_by_swap`)

359 gender-swapped copies of majority-lead stories are computed, balanced within each source so a large balanced source can't hide a skewed small one:

| source | female | male | neutral | added |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| african_storybook | 185 | 185 | 63 | 70 |
| storyweaver | 200 | 200 | 45 | 54 |
| tinystories | 9,482 | 9,482 | 1,271 | 235 |

Male and female leads are equal in every source after swapping.
Once the copies are added to the training data (KAN-80), the story LLM sees male and female leads equally often within each source.

**Trade-off / limits:** the swap is word-based ("her" → him/his and "his" → her/hers are heuristics) and only knows common names, so a few swapped stories read awkwardly. Copies are marked `augmented=True` so they can be kept out of evaluation and in the same split as their original (KAN-80).

## 3. Readability (CLEAR) by genre and era

4,723 excerpts. Teacher score `BT_easiness`: higher = easier. The Flesch-Kincaid grade (the baseline our readability scorer must beat) is mapped onto that score with a straight-line fit; overall MAE 0.708.

**By genre**

| genre | excerpts | mae | mean_error | teacher_score_mean |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Info | 2,304 | 0.771 | +0.106 | -1.268 |
| Lit | 2,419 | 0.647 | -0.100 | -0.662 |

**By publication era**

| era | excerpts | mae | mean_error | teacher_score_mean |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| before 1850 | 63 | 0.748 | +0.606 | -1.842 |
| 1850–1899 | 1,542 | 0.756 | +0.100 | -1.107 |
| 1900–1949 | 1,455 | 0.684 | +0.125 | -1.096 |
| 1950–1999 | 34 | 0.503 | +0.028 | -0.895 |
| 2000+ | 1,620 | 0.688 | -0.229 | -0.663 |
| unknown | 9 | 0.434 | -0.413 | -0.193 |

**Findings** (mean error > 0 means the formula rates the slice easier than teachers do)

- By era, the formula rates **1900–1949** texts easier than teachers do (+0.125) and **2000+** texts harder (-0.229); MAE differs by 0.072 across slices with at least 100 excerpts.
- By genre, the formula rates **Info** texts easier than teachers do (+0.106) and **Lit** texts harder (-0.100); MAE differs by 0.124 across slices with at least 100 excerpts.
- Too small to judge (under 100 excerpts): before 1850, 1950–1999, unknown.
- So a scorer trained on a random split could learn era and genre quirks instead of difficulty, and a single overall score would hide it.

**Mitigation: stratified splits** (`split_strata`): train/validation/test are split by genre × era so every slice appears in each split in the same proportion, and the scorer is evaluated per slice with the same `MetricFrame`, not just overall.

Strata with fewer than 100 excerpts are pooled into the nearest era of the same genre (excerpts without a year into the genre's largest era); 106 excerpts were moved. The split keys after pooling:

| Info / 1900–1949 | 459 |
| Info / 2000+ | 1,252 |
| Lit / 1850–1899 | 1,012 |
| Lit / 1900–1949 | 1,035 |

## What this does not cover yet

- The mitigations are computed and checked here, but not yet applied to the training data: the weights column, the augmented stories and the split assignment come with the training loaders (KAN-80).
- This report reads the current processed data; tying it to a frozen dataset version is KAN-81.
- Our own models are not trained yet. Once they are, the same slices are reused to measure them (recognizer per country, readability scorer per genre/era, story LLM output per lead gender).
- Country is the only demographic-like field; there is no age, gender or location of the people who drew. Children's drawings in the pilot are never sliced by child.
